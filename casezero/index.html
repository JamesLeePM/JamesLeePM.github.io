01 — The Problem

The Workflow Nobody Talks About

Before a real estate deal closes, someone has to research every lien, encumbrance, judgment, and cloud on the title. That someone is usually a paralegal or junior associate — spending hours in county recorder systems, manually cross-referencing names, dates, and legal descriptions.

The work is repetitive enough to be automated but consequential enough to require a licensed attorney to stand behind it. That gap is where most legal tech fails: they either automate too much (and create liability) or too little (and create nothing).

⏱

Time-Intensive

Manual lien searches across county systems, UCC filings, and judgment databases — hours of work per file, every closing.

⚠️

Error-Prone

Name variations, indexing errors, and multi-county properties create gaps that human review at speed consistently misses.

⚖️

Attorney Liability

A missed lien isn't just a deal problem — it's a malpractice problem. The attorney signs off. The attorney bears the risk.

📉

Margin Compression

High-volume closing firms run on thin margins. The hours burned in research don't scale — but the liability does.

The real insight: attorneys don't want AI to replace their judgment. They want AI to do the 80% of research that doesn't require judgment — so they can apply their expertise to the 20% that does.

02 — Design Principles

What We Decided Before We Built

Nine years in regulated federal environments taught me that the most important product decisions happen before the first sprint. CaseZero was designed around three non-negotiable constraints.

01

Attorney in the Loop — Always

No output that could reach a lienholder, client, or closing table is generated without an attorney review gate. The AI surfaces findings. The attorney certifies them. That line never moves.

02

Zero Hallucination Tolerance

In legal research, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. The system is designed to surface uncertainty explicitly — flagging gaps, conflicts, and low-confidence findings rather than filling them with plausible fiction.

03

Audit Trail Everything

Every AI-generated finding, every attorney review, every modification is timestamped and logged. If a closing unwinds two years later, the file tells the complete story of every decision and who made it.

03 — The Workflow

How the System Actually Works

The workflow was designed around how attorneys already think — not around what was easiest to build. Every stage has a clear owner: AI or human. There is no ambiguity about who is responsible for what.

1
Case Intake
Attorney or paralegal creates a new matter — property address, parties, transaction type. The system begins assembling the research scope: which jurisdictions, which record types, which name variants to search.
2
AI Research Phase
The system works through the research checklist — liens, judgments, UCC filings, tax records, lis pendens, and more. Each finding is sourced, dated, and confidence-scored. Gaps and ambiguities are flagged explicitly, not papered over.
3
Attorney Review Gate
The attorney sees every finding with its source and confidence score. They review, modify, escalate, or clear each item. Nothing proceeds without their explicit action on every flagged issue.
⚠ APPROVAL REQUIRED — attorney must clear all findings before output generation
4
Output Generation
Only after attorney sign-off does the system generate client-facing documents. The attorney's review is embedded in the audit trail — not as a formality, but as the actual decision record.
5
Closing File Archive
Every matter is archived with the complete research trail, attorney actions, and output versions. The file is self-documenting — built for the malpractice defense that may need it years later.
04 — Risk Architecture

Designing for Failure, Not Just Success

In a regulated domain, the most important design question isn't "how does this work when it works?" It's "what happens when it's wrong, and how do we know?" Every output type in CaseZero has an explicit failure mode and a designated human owner.

Output Type Risk Level Failure Mode Safeguard
Lien identification HIGH Missed encumbrance → closing liability Mandatory attorney review; confidence threshold gates
Name variant matching HIGH Wrong party → clean report on wrong person Attorney confirms party identity before scope is set
Document summarization MEDIUM Hallucinated clause → incorrect legal conclusion Source citation required; attorney reviews against original
Status interpretation MEDIUM Satisfied lien shown as active (or vice versa) Date/recording number always surfaced; attorney verifies currency
Timeline generation LOW Ordering error in chronology Automated cross-check against source dates
05 — What I Learned

The Decisions That Shaped the Product

The hardest part of building in a regulated domain isn't the technology — it's resisting the pressure to ship features that feel powerful but shift risk onto the wrong party.

→
Domain fluency before product fluency. I spent weeks inside actual closing workflows before writing a single spec. The product decisions that mattered most came from understanding what attorneys are actually afraid of — not from benchmarking other legal tech tools.
→
The approval gate is the product. Early instinct was to minimize friction. The right instinct was to make the review gate feel authoritative — because that's what makes attorneys trust the output. Friction at the right moment is a feature.
→
Uncertainty is more valuable than false confidence. "I found 3 liens and flagged 2 items I couldn't resolve" is a better answer than "I found 3 liens." Surfacing uncertainty is the trust mechanism — not a weakness to hide.
→
The audit trail isn't a compliance checkbox. It's the product's memory, its defensibility, and its differentiation. Building it right from day one — not as a log but as a decision record — changed how attorneys thought about what the system was actually doing for them.
→
Regulated industry buyers don't buy on capability — they buy on risk reduction. The pitch isn't "AI that finds liens faster." It's "a system designed so that if something goes wrong, you can show exactly what happened and why." That's a different product conversation entirely.

This is how I build.

Domain-first. Risk-aware. Designed for the human who signs off — not just the algorithm that generates the output.
