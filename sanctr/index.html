01 — The Problem

Why Most Health Apps Fail People

People don't lack health data. They lack health understanding. Lab results come back as numbers without context. Symptoms get Googled into spirals. Wearable data sits uninterpreted. Doctors have 12 minutes per visit.

The gap isn't information — it's the trusted, intelligent layer between raw health data and actionable understanding. And it has to be built in a way where users never have to wonder: what are you doing with my data?

🔬

Data Without Context

Lab values, vitals, and wearable metrics mean nothing without interpretation. Most people don't know if their A1C trend is alarming or normal.

🔒

Trust Deficit

Health apps have a track record of selling or mishandling personal data. Users know it. Adoption dies on arrival when trust isn't established from day one.

⏳

The 12-Minute Visit

Primary care is rationed. The intelligent layer that helps a person understand their health between visits doesn't exist — or is buried inside a walled garden.

🧩

Fragmented Data

Health data lives in five apps, two hospital portals, a wearable, and a paper folder. Nobody — including the patient — has the full picture.

The core insight: people will share their most sensitive data with an AI — but only if the product makes it undeniably clear that the data is working for them, not being harvested from them. Privacy isn't a feature. It's the business model.

02 — Design Principles

Built Around the Person, Not the Data

01

No PHI in Logs — Ever

Protected health information never appears in application logs, error traces, or telemetry. This isn't a policy — it's enforced at the architecture level. You can't accidentally log what the system never receives in plaintext.

02

AI as Interpreter, Not Diagnostician

Sanctr explains what health data means and surfaces patterns — it never diagnoses, prescribes, or replaces clinical judgment. The boundary is precise and non-negotiable. Users are always directed toward their provider for clinical decisions.

03

Transparency by Default

Every insight includes its source and confidence level. Users can always see what data an observation is based on. There are no black-box health scores — only explainable observations with clear sourcing.

03 — Privacy Architecture

How We Designed for Trust

Trust in a health product isn't built through a privacy policy — it's built through architecture decisions users can feel. Every layer of Sanctr is designed so that even if something goes wrong, the damage is bounded.

DATA INGESTION

Minimum Necessary Collection

We collect only what's needed to generate the insight the user asked for. No passive harvesting, no ambient data collection, no profile building for advertising.

PROCESSING

Ephemeral Context Windows

Health data passed to AI models is scoped to the user's session context. It is not stored in model memory, not used for training, and not retained after the response is generated.

LOGGING

Structural PHI Exclusion

Log pipelines are architected to structurally exclude health data fields. The exclusion is at the schema level — not a filter applied after the fact.

OUTPUT

Explainable, Source-Cited Insights

Every AI-generated insight includes what data it came from and what it is and isn't claiming. Users are never presented with a conclusion without a traceable basis.

USER CONTROL

Full Deletion, Real Deletion

When a user deletes their data, it's gone — from the database, from backups on a defined schedule, from everywhere the product touches. Not archived. Deleted.

04 — What I Learned

The Decisions That Shaped the Product

→
Nine years inside USPS Health Connect changed how I think about health products. I watched what happened when a healthcare product earned genuine trust at scale — White House recognition, 100% adoption, Surgeon General acknowledgment. Trust at scale requires architecture, not aspiration.
→
The AI-clinician boundary is the most important product decision in health tech. Crossing it even slightly — a recommendation that sounds clinical, a score that implies diagnosis — destroys trust and creates liability simultaneously. Precision matters more than capability.
→
Privacy as architecture beats privacy as policy. A privacy policy is a promise. A structural exclusion at the log schema level is a guarantee. Users who understand the difference trust the product. The ones who don't still benefit from the architecture.
→
Health users don't want to be managed — they want to be understood. The tone, the framing, and the output format matter as much as the accuracy. An insight delivered with clinical coldness gets ignored. The same insight framed for a real person gets acted on.

This is how I build.

Privacy by architecture. Intelligence with a boundary. Trust as the product, not the pitch.
