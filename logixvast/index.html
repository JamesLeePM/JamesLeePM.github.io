01 — The Problem

Logistics Runs on Instinct. It Shouldn't Have To.

The freight and logistics industry moves $900B+ annually in the US alone — and most operators are still making critical decisions with delayed data, disconnected systems, and experience-based intuition.

Large enterprises have supply chain visibility platforms. Everyone else has a TMS from 2009, a spreadsheet, and a dispatcher who's been there 20 years. The intelligence gap between big and small in logistics is enormous — and it's where margin disappears.

📊

Delayed Visibility

Shipment status, carrier performance, and cost data arrive hours or days after the decisions that needed them. Operators are always flying blind on yesterday's information.

🔗

Disconnected Systems

TMS, ERP, carrier portals, and tracking systems don't talk to each other. The full picture of an operation exists only in someone's head — and leaves when they do.

💸

Hidden Cost Leakage

Accessorial charges, detention fees, and fuel surcharges accrue invisibly until the invoice arrives. By then it's too late to dispute or route around them.

🧠

Institutional Knowledge Risk

The best decisions live in experienced dispatchers' heads. When they leave, the intelligence leaves with them. There's no system capturing the patterns that make operations efficient.

The core insight: logistics operators don't need another dashboard — they need a system that surfaces the right signal at the moment a decision is being made. Intelligence at the point of action, not intelligence in a report viewed after the fact.

02 — Design Principles

Built for the Operator, Not the Analyst

01

Decision Support, Not Decision Replacement

Experienced logistics operators have hard-won instincts. The system augments those instincts with real-time data — it doesn't override them. Every AI recommendation surfaces its reasoning so the operator can agree, override, or learn from it.

02

Real-Time or Useless

In freight, a routing decision made on 4-hour-old data is a bad decision. The data model is built around real-time ingestion and event-driven updates — not batch reporting cycles that arrive after the truck has already departed.

03

Institutional Knowledge Capture

Every decision made in the platform — and its outcome — builds a layer of operational intelligence. Over time, the system learns the patterns that make a specific operation efficient, creating a knowledge asset that outlasts any individual employee.

03 — Intelligence Layers

What the Platform Sees

LogixVast is designed around four intelligence layers that compound on each other. Each layer is useful in isolation; together they create a picture of an operation that no single system or person could maintain manually.

Shipment Visibility

Real-time location, status, and ETA across carriers and modes. Exceptions surfaced before they become problems — not after the delivery window has closed.

Carrier Performance

On-time rates, damage frequency, and cost consistency tracked per carrier, lane, and time period. Selection intelligence that improves with every shipment.

Cost Intelligence

Accessorial charges, detention patterns, and fuel surcharge trends surfaced in real time. Dispute triggers flagged automatically before invoices are approved.

Route Optimization

AI-assisted routing that accounts for carrier capacity, historical lane performance, and current freight market conditions — not just distance and price.

Demand Forecasting

Pattern recognition across order history, seasonality, and external signals to help operators anticipate capacity needs before the surge hits.

Operations Memory

Every decision and outcome archived as institutional knowledge. New dispatchers start with the accumulated intelligence of everyone who came before them.

04 — What I Learned

The Decisions That Shaped the Product

→
Logistics operators are skeptical of technology for good reason. They've been sold platforms that required 6-month implementations and a dedicated IT team. The product had to be deployable fast and produce visible value within the first week — or it would be shelved like every other system before it.
→
The dispatcher is the product's most important user — and the hardest to win. Experienced dispatchers have seen companies try to automate their job. The pitch isn't "AI will do what you do." It's "AI will handle the noise so you can focus on the decisions that actually require your judgment."
→
Real-time data architecture is a product decision, not just a technical one. Choosing event-driven over batch processing changed the entire user experience — from a reporting tool operators check at the end of the day, to an intelligence layer they keep open during operations. Same data. Completely different product.
→
The margin story sells faster than the efficiency story. "Save 12% on freight costs" lands differently than "improve visibility." Logistics buyers think in margin. The product speaks their language — not the language of software demos.
